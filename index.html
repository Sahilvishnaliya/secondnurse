The nurse who watches your hands when the nurse isn’t there.

A nurse trains a dialysis patient once. Then they perform a sterile procedure alone, at home, for years — and the commonest cause of infection is a half-second touch nobody saw. SecondNurse points a phone at the bench, tracks every hand and sterile part as a pixel mask, and stops the procedure the second sterile technique breaks.

    How it works

    Contamination, defined as geometry — not an AI’s opinion.

    01

    Muse Wonder compiles the protocol

    A written procedure goes in. A machine-checkable step graph comes out: steps, required objects, forbidden contacts, minimum disinfection times. New procedure, new document — zero new code.

    PDF → StepGraph JSON
    02

    SAM 3.1 tracks masks, not guesses

    Text prompts return pixel-precise masks for hands, cap, connector and sterile field, with identity preserved through the video. A vision-language model guesses; a mask measures.

    sam-3.1 · up to 16 objects
    03

    A deterministic engine calls the breach

    Hand mask overlapping the sterile tip for over 0.3s is a touch contamination. Cap outside the field is a field breach. Skipped steps are order breaches. Thresholds you can defend, events you can replay.

    overlap ≥ 40px² · ≥ 0.3s

    Safety & scope

    What this is, and what it is not.

    It is a training simulator

    Simulated kit, simulated procedure, technique feedback for education. It detects the technique break a nurse trainer watches for — it cannot see bacteria, and it never claims to.

    A human stays in charge

    The system flags; a person decides. Every alert is reviewable with its frames, and session reports go to the care team. No diagnosis, no treatment decision, no autonomous clinical action.

    Private by construction

    Demo mode processes geometry locally in the browser. No patient data is collected, stored, or sent anywhere. Live mode sends frames only to the Meta Model API you configure.
