Safety & Scope

SecondNurse works next to a medical procedure, so its boundaries are stated plainly, in the product, in these docs, and in the code.

What SecondNurse is

  • An educational training system: a physical training kit, a real procedure performed on that kit, live technique feedback.
  • A technique watcher: it detects the same events a nurse trainer watches for — touches, boundary breaks, skipped steps.
  • A session record: events with frame evidence, reviewable by the patient and their care team.

What SecondNurse is not

  • Not a medical device. It has no clinical validation and no regulatory clearance.
  • It cannot see bacteria. It detects the technique break that risks contamination — a proxy, stated as such.
  • It does not diagnose infection or any condition, and never recommends treatment (including antibiotics) or clinical action.
  • It does not replace training. A qualified nurse teaches the procedure; SecondNurse watches practice afterwards.

Human in charge

Consistent with Meta's Model API Acceptable Use Policy, outputs never make decisions affecting a person's health on their own. The system flags a technique break; the patient corrects it; a qualified person reviews flagged sessions. Alerts say “stop and correct the technique” — never “you are infected” or “proceed”.

Data handling

  • Camera frames are sent only to the Meta Model API you configured, for segmentation. SecondNurse stores no images or video.
  • No real patient data is used in development or testing; the bench is a training kit.
  • Session logs are event text and timestamps, kept in the browser session.
  • API keys are server-side environment variables, never exposed to the client or the repository.

Known limitations

  • Occlusion: a hand covering the tip is both the event and the hardest frame to segment. Consecutive-frame confirmation and box-prompt tracking mitigate; they do not eliminate.
  • Latency: seconds per analyzed frame. Suitable for “stop before you connect”, not for intercepting fast motion.
  • Kit dependence: reliability is engineered around colour-coded, matte, generously sized training parts. Clinical hardware would need its own validation set.
  • Model variance: SAM and Muse are upstream services; a step graph must be human-reviewed, and a missed detection must never be treated as proof of sterility.

The rule that governs all of it

SecondNurse may say “that touch happened.” It may never say “you are safe.” Absence of an alert is absence of a detection, not a guarantee of sterility.
